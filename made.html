01

I wanted to know where the planes above me were going.

SkyPing

I kept seeing planes above Pune and wondering where they were going. So I made my Mac tell me.

SkyPing watches the sky around a location and sends a notification when an aircraft appears, along with where it’s coming from and where it’s headed.

There wasn’t a productivity problem to solve. I was just curious.

02

What if papers were designed to be understood?

PaperLens

Research papers contain beautiful ideas hidden behind terrible interfaces.

I built PaperLens to turn an arXiv paper into something closer to an interactive textbook: intuition first, then diagrams, concepts, mathematics, and the full technical explanation.

The interesting problem wasn’t summarization. It was deciding how an idea should reveal itself to someone learning it.

03

AI that belongs to the device.

PocketLlama

What if your AI didn’t need the internet, an account, or permission from a server?

PocketLlama is a small language model that lives entirely on your phone. Download the model once and conversations happen locally. No API key, no usage meter, no chat leaving the device.

I like the idea that some software should simply belong to the person holding it.

04

“Done” is a claim.

Redpen

Coding agents learned to say “done” before they learned to prove it. So I built Redpen.

It takes what an agent claims it accomplished and compares those claims against independent evidence from the repository: actual changes, tests, builds, and other deterministic checks.

The last state is my favorite. Good software should be allowed to say: I don’t know.

A little about how I make

Questions first.
Frameworks second.

I don’t think of software as a collection of frameworks. I like starting with a question and finding the smallest thing that makes the idea real.

Sometimes that’s a language model running on a phone. Sometimes it’s a terminal command. Sometimes it’s a notification that tells me where the plane above my head is going.

I’m interested in AI, tools, interfaces, learning, and strange little pieces of software that make computers feel more personal.

I want to spend more of my life making them.
